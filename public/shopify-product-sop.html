1. How the planner sees your shop

Think of the planner as a live mirror of the Brown Box Kit Shopify shop, not a separate copy. Every time a customer opens the planner, it asks Shopify “what products do you have right now?” and shows whatever comes back. So you never push or sync anything — you just make sure each product is set up correctly in Shopify, then reload the planner.

For a product to appear and look right, it needs three things in Shopify:

  • Be switched on for the planner (the right sales channel).
  • Have a final title (no “(Draft)” in the name).
  • Have its five size fields filled in (the planner metafields), plus a price.
Industry note (why we do it this way) The big flat-pack planners work the same way. Kaboodle (Bunnings) treats cabinets, doors, panels and benchtops as separate products that combine into one item list. IKEA treats a cabinet as a frame and auto-adds the matching parts (doors, hinges, legs). We're following the same proven model — a cabinet on screen is really a small kit of real products.

2. Part A — Make a product show up correctly Do this now

Do these five steps for every product you want in the planner.

Step 1 — Publish it to the planner's sales channel

  1. In Shopify admin, open the product.
  2. Find the Publishing / Sales channels box on the right.
  3. Make sure the channel the planner uses is ticked. (Confirm the exact channel name with Brandon once — it's the same for every product.)
  4. To do many at once: Products list → tick the products → More actions → Add available channel → choose that channel.

This is the #1 reason a new product doesn't appear.

Step 2 — Make the title final (no “Draft”)

The planner hides any product with (Draft) in its title, so works-in-progress stay out of the customer's view. When the product is ready, remove “(Draft)” from the title.

Step 3 — Fill in the five planner size fields (metafields)

On the product page, scroll to Metafields and set all five fields in the planner group. Take the numbers from the cabinet spec sheet, measured in millimetres.

FieldWhat to enterExample
planner.glb_urlWeb link to the 3D model filehttps://…/base-600.glb
planner.width_mmWidth in mm (number only)600
planner.height_mmHeight in mm (number only)720
planner.depth_mmDepth in mm (number only)560
planner.categoryCabinet typebase, wall, tall, corner, island
Important If you leave the size fields blank, the planner guesses 600 × 580 × 720 mm and shows a plain box. A cabinet that looks the wrong size means these numbers are missing or wrong — fix them and reload.

Step 4 — Check the price and SKU

The product (or its variant) needs a price in NZD and a SKU code. The planner shows the price and uses the SKU/variant when the customer sends the design to the cart.

Step 5 — Verify in the planner

  1. Open the planner and reload the page (Ctrl + R on computer, or pull-to-refresh on mobile).
  2. Find the product in the catalog list.
  3. Confirm: correct name, correct size (e.g. 600 × 560 × 720mm), a price, and a real 3D model (not “⬜ placeholder model”).
  4. Place it in a test plan and check it looks right. If it's wrong, go back to the matching field in Step 3.

3. Part B — Record each cabinet's parts list Prep for later

Goal: each cabinet should eventually pull in its door, benchtop and hardware automatically — like Kaboodle's full item list — so customers get a complete, honest shopping list. That feature isn't switched on yet, so for now start gathering the data in a simple spreadsheet. When the feature ships, we drop this straight in and it works immediately.

For each cabinet (carcase) product, record:

ColumnWhat to putExample
Cabinet nameThe carcase product600 Base Cabinet
Cabinet SKUIts product/variant codeBBK-BASE-600
Door SKUThe matching door panel's codeBBK-DOOR-600
Door qtyUsually 1 per cabinet1
Hardware SKU(s)Hinges / handles, if sold separatelyBBK-HINGE ×2
Benchtop SKUThe benchtop product codeBBK-BENCH-LAM
Benchtop ruleBy length — note this; it's not 1 eachper metre of cabinet width
How the quantities work
  • Doors & hardware = a fixed number per cabinet (simple — this is the first thing we'll switch on).
  • Benchtop = by length, never “one per cabinet”. Three 600 mm cabinets in a row share one 1800 mm benchtop. We're finalising exactly how the planner measures benchtop length before automating it — the same way IKEA and Kaboodle always have a person confirm the benchtop.
